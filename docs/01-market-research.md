# Market research: fantasy photoshoots & wardrobe rental, Stockholm

Written 2026-09-05. Everything with a number attached has a source at the bottom.
Anything marked **[verify]** is my inference, not a sourced fact: check it before betting money on it.

---

## 1. The short version

**The product can work here, but not as a translation of the Vietnamese product.** Three findings drive everything else:

1. **The demand exists, but it is not the demand you have in Vietnam.** Swedes already spend real money on identity photography: maternity, newborn, graduation, weddings, dogs. What they do *not* buy is status display. If you market "look prestigious," you will lose. If you market "transformation, play, and a rite of passage," you are selling something Sweden already buys.

2. **The Nordic fantasy canon is a gift and you are not using it.** Every image on the mood board is East Asian fantasy. Sweden has its own deep, non-embarrassing forest-folklore tradition: skogsrå, huldra, vittra, näcken, John Bauer's illustrations, Midsommar flower crowns. A Swede looking at your board sees "Chinese costume drama." A Swede looking at a skogsrå shoot sees *their own childhood.* Same craft, same wings, completely different cultural permission.

3. **The economics only work if you batch.** At any realistic Stockholm price, one client per day with a paid makeup artist leaves you almost nothing. The Vietnamese studios you are copying run 3–6 clients through one set-up per day. That structural fact should shape the test run, not just the pricing page.

**Verdict:** worth the test run. Do not sign a lease, do not buy 50,000 kr of costumes, and do not incorporate before the numbers in [02-validation-plan.md](02-validation-plan.md) come back.

## 2. What you are actually selling

You framed it as "fantasy photoshoots." That is the *format*, not the product. Underneath, customers buy one of four different things, and they have very different price ceilings:

| What they're buying | Emotional job | Price ceiling | Sweden fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A milestone marker** | "This chapter of my life deserves a record" | High: people already pay 6–12k for newborn shoots | ★★★★★ |
| **A transformation experience** | "For three hours I was someone else" | Medium-high, and it's an *event*, not a purchase | ★★★★☆ |
| **Social content** | "I need images that make my feed look good" | Low-medium, price-sensitive, high churn | ★★☆☆☆ |
| **Craft/portfolio assets** | Cosplayers, LARPers, musicians, authors | Low: they're used to trading for free | ★★☆☆☆ |

The Vietnamese market is dominated by column 3. **In Sweden, column 3 is your weakest column and column 1 is your strongest.** That inversion is the single most important thing in this document.

## 3. Does the pain point exist, or is it FOMO?

Honestly: for the *social content* framing, it is mostly FOMO, and Swedish FOMO is weaker than Vietnamese FOMO. You were right to suspect this.

**Why the Vietnamese/Chinese version works so well at home:**
- A dense visual-status culture where appearing well-presented is socially rewarded, not punished
- Extremely low labour cost, so a full-service shoot with MUA, stylist and assistant costs a fraction of what it costs here
- Studios clustered and competing, which normalised the product: people know it exists and know roughly what it costs
- Photo-sharing platforms (Xiaohongshu especially) where a styled shoot is *content*, not bragging

**What transfers to Sweden:**
- The universal part: people want to see themselves looking beautiful. Sweden's maternity/newborn photography market proves Swedes will pay four figures for exactly that.
- Dressing up is genuinely normalised here: arguably more than in Vietnam. Sweden has the largest cosplay festival in the Nordics (NärCon, ~17,000 visitors), the country's biggest historical festival where a whole town dresses medieval (Medeltidsveckan, 60–70,000 visitors), a serious LARP tradition, plus maskerad, Halloween and Valborg. Several long-established costume rental houses survive on this demand, one of them with 1,600 m² of stock.
- Nature-romanticism is a core part of Swedish self-image. A forest shoot is not exotic here, it is *homely*.

**What does not transfer:**
- **Jantelagen.** The norm against acting as though you are better than others. It is weakening, especially online, but it is still the filter through which a Swede judges whether a picture of themselves is lovely or embarrassing. This is not a reason to abandon the idea: it is a reason to change the framing and the retouching.
- The "capture your youth before it fades" pitch. It reads as anxious rather than aspirational here.
- Heavy skin retouching. In Vietnamese studio output, smooth skin signals quality. In Sweden it signals fake. **[verify with your own audience test]**: this is my strongest aesthetic claim and the cheapest one to test.

**The reframe that I think works:**

> Not *"look like a princess."*
> But *"one afternoon, we make you into something out of a Swedish fairy tale, and you keep the pictures."*

Play, craft and folklore. Not status.

## 4. Target segments, ranked

Ranked by **(willingness to pay) × (ease of reaching them) × (how much they market you for free)**.

### S1: Beachhead: Asian diaspora and international students in Stockholm
Roughly 40,000 Chinese-born residents in Sweden, heavily concentrated in Stockholm, plus Vietnamese and Korean communities and a large international student population.

- They already know what this product is. **Zero education cost**: the hardest cost to pay.
- They already use the platforms where this content spreads (Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Instagram).
- They will pay, because they know the home price and know Sweden is expensive.
- **The trap:** this segment is finite, and if all your portfolio is East Asian fantasy shot on East Asian clients, you will find it very hard to cross to Swedish customers later. Serve them, take their money, but shoot Swedish-facing work *in parallel from day one.*

### S2: Milestone women, 25–40
The proven paying segment in Sweden. Maternity/newborn photography here is a mature market with established prop-and-styling norms and clients routinely landing between 6,000 and 12,000 kr.

- **"Fantasy maternity"** is the highest-margin idea in this document. The segment already pays, already accepts elaborate styling, and nobody in Stockholm is doing it as a forest-goddess concept.
- Adjacent: 30th/40th birthdays, divorce/"rebirth" shoots, mother–daughter shoots.
- **Möhippa (hen parties)** deserve their own line. Swedish hen parties are ritualised, budgeted for, group-purchased, and explicitly licence silly dressing-up. A 5-person fantasy shoot as a möhippa activity solves your batching problem and your Jantelagen problem at once: nobody feels vain when the whole group is in costume.

### S3: Cosplay / LARP / historical reenactment
NärCon at ~17,000 visitors, Medeltidsveckan at 60–70,000, an active national LARP scene.

- Highest enthusiasm, **lowest price ceiling.** These are hobbyists used to photographers shooting them for free in exchange for portfolio.
- Their real value to you is **content and credibility**, not revenue, and as **rental customers**, which is a different and better business line (see §7).

### S4: Teens and young adults, parents paying
Studentfotografering (graduation) and konfirmation are institutionalised Swedish photo purchases. A fantasy alternative to the standard graduation portrait is a real wedge, but it is a seasonal, once-a-year window (May–June) and you would be competing with entrenched school contracts.

### S5: Creative professionals
Musicians, authors, tattoo artists, small brands needing fantasy imagery. Low volume, good day rates, excellent portfolio. Worth taking, not worth targeting.

### S6: Tourists
Stockholm has serious tourist volume, and "shoot a Nordic fairy tale in a real Swedish forest" is a genuinely appealing thing to sell to visiting East Asian tourists. Highly seasonal (May–Sept), needs a booking channel you do not have yet. **Park this for year two.**

## 5. Competitive landscape

**Direct competitors: effectively none in Stockholm.** I could not find a productised fantasy/costume photoshoot service here: only one-off stylised shoots by individual photographers.

That is genuinely a white space. It is also the thing that should worry you most: **white space sometimes means nobody wants it.** Ten years of photographers in a rich city have not built this business. Assume there is a reason, and make finding that reason the job of the test run.

**The adjacent players you will actually compete with:**

| Who | What they do | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Portrait/family/maternity studios | 895 kr mini-sessions up to 12,000 kr newborn packages | They set the price anchor in the customer's head |
| Costume rental houses (Independent Kostym, Yippo, La Petite Mort, Karl Gerhards, Maskeradateljén) | Weekly costume rental, 1,600 m² of stock at the largest | **Rent from them before you buy.** They are also your competitor if you want to run a rental line |
| Studio rental spaces | 900 kr/2h to 9,000 kr/day | Your indoor fallback, and your winter production base |
| Cosplay photographers | Often free or near-free | They cap what the fandom segment will pay |

**Your defensible edge is not the camera.** Stockholm has hundreds of competent photographers. Your edge is **wardrobe + hair/makeup + set + art direction as one package**: the operational chain most photographers cannot be bothered to build. Protect that; it is the moat.

## 6. Pricing

**Stockholm benchmarks (all incl. VAT unless noted):**

| Thing | Price |
|---|---|
| Studio mini-session, 30 min / 5 images | 895 kr |
| Full-day studio session, unlimited images | 4,495 kr |
| Portrait session 1–2h, 20–40 delivered | 6,000–9,000 kr |
| Professional photographer, hourly | 1,200–2,500 kr **ex VAT** |
| Smallest maternity package w/ 5 retouched images | 4,950 kr |
| Typical newborn client total spend | 6,000–12,000 kr |
| Hair + makeup add-on at an existing studio | 1,600 kr |
| Studio rental, 2h | from 900 kr ex VAT |

**Proposed ladder, a hypothesis to test rather than a decision:**

| Package | Price | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Prova på** (open shoot day, 90-min slot) | 2,900 kr | Shared set-up day, one look, group MUA, 10 retouched |
| **Signature** (private session) | 6,900 kr | Own date, one look, full hair/MUA, wardrobe, 20 retouched |
| **Dubbel** | 9,900 kr | Two looks / two locations, 35 retouched |
| **Sällskap** (2–5 people, möhippa) | 2,400 kr per person, min 3 | One set-up, shared MUA, 12 retouched each |
| Add-ons | | Extra look +1,800 · extra retouch 250/image · print box +1,200 · wings hire +600 |

Rationale: you must land **above** the maternity-studio anchor, because you are adding wardrobe, MUA and location that they do not include, and the price has to be visibly justified by craft the customer can see in the first three seconds of your portfolio. If your images do not look worth 6,900 kr, the answer is not to drop the price, it is to not launch yet.

## 7. Unit economics: read this part twice

**One private client, everything bought in:**

| Line | SEK |
|---|---|
| Price (incl. 25% VAT) | 6,900 |
| Revenue ex VAT | 5,520 |
| Makeup artist (3–4h) | −1,800 |
| Assistant / second pair of hands | −800 |
| Flowers + consumables | −400 |
| Wardrobe & prop amortisation | −500 |
| Transport, parking | −250 |
| Location fee or permit (varies) | −0 to 800 |
| **Contribution before your own labour** | **≈ 1,000–1,800** |

For roughly 12 hours of combined work across shooting, styling, editing and admin. **That is not a business, that is a hobby with receipts.**

**Same day, batched: four clients, one location set-up, one MUA booking:**

| Line | SEK |
|---|---|
| 4 × 2,900 kr, ex VAT | 9,280 |
| MUA, full day | −4,500 |
| Assistant, full day | −1,600 |
| Flowers, consumables, transport | −900 |
| Wardrobe amortisation | −800 |
| **Contribution** | **≈ 1,480** |

Also thin, because the open-day price is low. **The version that works is the blend:** run batched days to fill the calendar and generate portfolio and word of mouth, and sell 2–4 private Signature/Dubbel sessions a month at full price on top. And do the makeup yourselves for as long as you can stand to; the MUA line is the single biggest cost you control.

**Scale reality check.** To reach 1,000,000 kr of annual revenue at a 5,500 kr average, you need roughly **180 shoots a year: about 15 a month, four a week.** Look at that number honestly and decide whether you want it, because it is the actual shape of the business. Everything before that number is a side project.

**The rental line is quietly the better business.** Renting wings, dresses and headpieces to cosplayers, LARPers, other photographers and Medeltidsveckan-goers has near-zero marginal cost, no labour per transaction, and monetises the wardrobe you are buying anyway. It smooths the brutal Swedish winter season. Do not treat it as an afterthought.

## 8. Seasonality: the constraint nobody plans for

| Period | Daylight | What you can sell |
|---|---|---|
| Late Apr – Aug | Long, soft, endless | Peak outdoor. Midsommar flower crowns. Tourists. Möhippor. |
| **Sep – mid Oct** | Sunset 18:50 → 17:30, autumn colour | **Best light of the year. Your test run window is well chosen.** |
| Late Oct – Nov | Sunset before 16:00, wet, grey | Dead outdoors. Studio only. |
| Dec – Feb | ~6h daylight | Snow/ice-queen concepts if snow cooperates; otherwise studio, headpieces, rental income |
| Mar – Apr | Returning light, ugly ground | Studio, planning, marketing build |

Roughly **five months of good outdoor production a year.** Any plan that ignores this fails in November. This is also the strongest argument for building the studio-glam concept and the rental line early rather than "later."

## 9. Legal and admin: flag list, not advice

Verify all of this with an accountant or Skatteverket. I am flagging what to ask about, not answering it.

- **Company form.** Enskild firma is cheap and instant; aktiebolag needs share capital (25,000 kr) and gives you liability separation. With three founders, a written agreement about ownership and who owns the images matters more than the form. **[verify]**
- **F-skatt** registration before you invoice anyone.
- **VAT.** Photography services are generally 25%. There are reduced rates for certain artistic/photographic works. Do not guess: the difference materially changes your pricing. **[verify]**
- **Commercial shooting in nature.** This one is a real operational risk and most photographers get it wrong: **allemansrätten does not extend to commercial activity.** Nature reserves and national parks have their own regulations, and organised commercial use typically needs permission from the landowner or the county administrative board. For the test run this is low-stakes, but the moment you are running paid client shoots weekly in the same reserve, sort it out.
- **Model releases + GDPR.** You need written, specific consent to use client images in marketing, separate from the shoot contract, and a clean way for people to withdraw it. Many clients will *want* to be featured: get it in writing anyway.
- **Insurance.** Equipment and liability. A client twisting an ankle on a mossy rock in Tyresta is not a hypothetical.

## 10. Risks, and what would kill this

| Risk | Severity | Early warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Swedes find the aesthetic embarrassing, not beautiful | **Fatal** | Diaspora bookings fine, Swedish enquiries near zero after 8 weeks of content |
| Output quality doesn't justify 6,900 kr | **Fatal** | You look at your own test-run gallery and privately wouldn't pay for it |
| Unit economics never clear founder labour | High | Third month running, contribution per shoot still under 2,000 kr |
| Five-month season starves cash Nov–Mar | High | No studio or rental revenue by October |
| Founder capacity: three people, no full-timer | High | Test run slips twice |
| AI image generation eats the low end | Medium | Enquiries asking "can't you just AI this?" |

**Kill criteria: agree these now, while it's cheap to be honest.** If after the test run and eight weeks of marketing you have fewer than 5 paid bookings and fewer than 2 of them are Swedish-born customers, the Swedish thesis is unproven and you should either pivot to serving the diaspora deliberately as a small business, or stop.

## 11. What I would actually do

1. **Run the test run** as planned, but treat it as a *product test*, not a portfolio shoot. See [03-test-run-plan.md](03-test-run-plan.md).
2. **Shoot both aesthetics on the same day.** One East Asian fantasy look, one Nordic folklore look, same model, same location, same hour. Post both. Let the audience tell you which market you are in. This is the highest-information thing you can do for zero extra cost.
3. **Rent the wardrobe for the test run, don't buy it.** Independent Kostym and the others exist precisely so you don't have to commit capital before you know what you need.
4. **Sell 6–8 founding-client slots** at a discount in exchange for full usage rights and an honest recorded interview afterwards. Real money changing hands is the only validation that counts.
5. **Do not build the full website yet.** A one-page site with prices, ten images and a booking form is enough to test demand. (Nam: hosting discussion is separate.)
